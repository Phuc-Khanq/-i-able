# big3s16lr: half the learning rate, the first run to beat big1

big3s16lr is the afternoon run of 2026-11-05 (14:02 to 16:33). It's big3s16 with **one change: learning rate 1e-4
instead of 2e-4**. Everything else is the same: rank 16 / alpha 32, the data (`data/cleanup_train/big3s_train.jsonl`,
8,000 examples, 3,153 different answers), the seed, the split (7,598 trained, the same 402 held back as whole sentences),
the order, 2 epochs, 1,898 steps, warm-up and cosine decay. Its first loss check matches big3s16's to four decimals.
149.7 min on the 5080, peak 9.66 GB. big3s16 took 182.1 min, but the PC was busy then, so the time says nothing about
the learning rate. Scoring as the app runs it: llama.cpp b11960, Q8_0, greedy, prompt `cleanup_t1_short`, Khang's PC
(RTX 5080). The test file is the unchanged big1_test.csv (sha256 ec2b1b0aa648c0e3).

Head to head on the fair 2,301 (automatic, not hand-checked with Khang's rule):

- **Against big1: epoch 1 wins 10 and loses 4; the final adapter wins 9 and loses 3.**
- Against big3s16 (only the learning rate differs): the final adapter wins 8 and loses 4.
- Epoch 1 against the final adapter: 3 each way, and the same answer word for word on 2,424 of 2,500.

**How sure is this?** The leads are small: +5 on the 2,500 and +6 on the fair 2,301. A 9-to-3 split could still come
from chance (about 15% of the time if the two were really equal). What makes it believable is that every count points
the same way except one (the 199 cases with terms only big1 trained on: 163 vs 166), and so do the loss checks below.
The hidden test decides.

It nearly closes big1's lead on the 199 cases with terms big1 trained on and big3s's data didn't (165 vs 166), without
training on those terms. Of the 94 cases whose terms no run trained on, it passes 89 (big1 84).

## The loss checks: smaller steps learn faster

Loss with dropout off, every 500 steps (step 949 is the end of epoch 1):

| Step | Training, 1e-4 | Training, 2e-4 | Held-back sentences, 1e-4 | Held-back sentences, 2e-4 | Test cases, 1e-4 | Test cases, 2e-4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.0943 | 0.0943 | 0.0953 | 0.0953 | 0.1062 | 0.1062 |
| 500 | **0.0111** | 0.0163 | **0.0163** | 0.0243 | **0.0243** | 0.0289 |
| 949 | **0.0021** | 0.0031 | **0.0129** | 0.0156 | **0.0192** | 0.0239 |
| 1,000 | **0.0021** | 0.0027 | **0.0126** | 0.0155 | **0.0187** | 0.0239 |
| 1,500 | 0.0010 | 0.0010 | 0.0139 | 0.0133 | 0.0197 | 0.0195 |
| 1,898 | 0.0004 | 0.0006 | 0.0135 | 0.0133 | 0.0199 | 0.0200 |

- **Half the learning rate learns faster, not slower.** At step 500 all three losses are lower than at 2e-4, the training
  loss included. That fits the guess from big3s16: the steps were too big and overshot. (Rank 32 at the same alpha
  ratio made them bigger still.)
- **Its best point is the end of epoch 1.** The test-case loss at step 1,000 is 0.0187: the lowest of any adapter we've
  measured (big1's final 0.0190, big3s16's 0.0200). In epoch 2 both new-data losses rise a little (test cases 0.0187 to
  0.0199, held-back sentences 0.0126 to 0.0135) while the training loss keeps falling (0.0021 to 0.0004). That is
  textbook overfitting, though small, and the pass rate doesn't improve in epoch 2 (2,446 to 2,444).
- At the end, both learning rates land in the same place (0.0199 vs 0.0200). The difference is in how they got there.
- The gap to the training loss is still about 7 times at epoch 1 (0.0129 vs 0.0021). Our data generator's habits are
  still being learned.

## What it means

1. **Use learning rate 1e-4 from now on**, with rank 16. It's ahead of or level with big3s16 on every automatic count,
   and of big1 on every count but the 199 with big1's own terms. The loss checks show why: the old steps were too big.
2. **A second epoch doesn't help at 1e-4.** Both new-data losses are lowest at the end of epoch 1 and rise slightly
   after. This matters for 100,000 examples: if one epoch is enough, training time halves. Not proven yet: the epoch 1
   adapter of a two-epoch run stops halfway down the cosine decay, so it isn't the same as a one-epoch run.
3. **New candidate for the hidden test: big3s16lr epoch 1.** It's ahead of big1 everywhere, but by a little. Swapping
   the app's model file needs Khang's OK. Pick one model before running the hidden test.
4. **The three-times-data test should be re-run with these settings.** "More data made it worse" was measured at rank 32
   and 2e-4. With rank 16 and 1e-4, big3's 24,000 against big3s's 8,000 changes only the data.
