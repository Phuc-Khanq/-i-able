# big3s: the first run that measures over- and underfitting

big3s is the evening run of 2026-11-04: a LoRA adapter on Qwen3.5-4B, rank 32 / alpha 64 (as big2), 2 epochs, trained
on 8,000 random examples of big3's data (`data/cleanup_train/big3s_train.jsonl`, seed 8000; 3,153 different answers).
402 examples were held back as **whole sentences** it never trains on (`--held-back-by sentence`), so 7,598 trained.
It ran 1,898 steps in 175.4 min on the 5080 (peak 10.21 GB). Every 500 steps, the trainer measured the loss with
dropout off on 300 training examples, on the held-back sentences and on 400 cases of the 2,500-case test
(`big1_test_watch.jsonl`, measured only, never trained on). Scoring as the app runs it: llama.cpp b11960, Q8_0, greedy,
prompt `cleanup_t1_short`, Khang's PC (RTX 5080). The test file is the unchanged big1_test.csv (sha256 ec2b1b0aa648c0e3).

## Result: big3s doesn't beat big1. big1 stays the candidate.

| | big1 | big2 | big3s epoch 1 | big3s final |

| big1 test, 2,500, automatic | **2,441** | 2,411 | 2,424 | 2,418 |
| The 2,301 cases with no term that only big1 trained on | **2,275** | 2,254 | 2,266 | 2,262 |
| The 94 cases whose terms no run trained on | 84 | 83 | 84 | 84 |
| Clean sentences changed (of 378) | **4** | 14 | 7 | 7 |

big3s's final answers are word for word big1's on 2,280 of the 2,500. On the fair 2,301, big1 passes 17 that big3s
fails, and big3s passes 4 that big1 fails. Not hand-checked with Khang's rule.

## The loss checks (the new part)

| Step | Training examples | Held-back sentences | Test cases |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 (before) | 0.0943 | 0.0953 | 0.1062 |
| 500 | 0.0156 | 0.0226 | 0.0293 |
| 949 (end of epoch 1) | 0.0029 | 0.0206 | 0.0268 |
| 1,000 | 0.0032 | 0.0189 | 0.0295 |
| 1,500 | 0.0016 | 0.0176 | 0.0211 |
| 1,898 (end) | 0.0009 | 0.0170 | 0.0208 |

- **Not underfitting.** The training loss ends at 0.0009: the network learns its training data almost perfectly.
- **Not overfitting in the usual sense, within 2 epochs.** The loss on sentences it never saw falls at every check and
  never rises. On the test cases it also falls, except for one bump at step 1,000.
- **But a large gap.** At the end, the loss on new sentences is 19 times the training loss (0.0170 vs 0.0009). 93% of
  the fall on new sentences happens in the first 500 steps (0.0953 to 0.0226). After that, the training loss falls
  another 17 times (0.0156 to 0.0009), but the loss on new sentences falls only by a quarter (0.0226 to 0.0170). So
  after step 500, almost all the training is memorizing.
- This held-back loss (whole sentences) can't be compared with big1's 0.0094 or big2's 0.0040. Those were held back
  by example, and noisy copies of the same sentence were on both sides.

## What the errors look like (the 17 lost on the fair 2,301, and the others)

- **Spoken numbers turned into digits:** "two point one" -> "2.1" and "2.0.1", "three fifty" -> "350 degrees". The
  data doesn't teach this: 1 answer in big3s's 8,000 has digits that the heard words didn't (a "1099" form). It's the
  base model's habit coming back.
- **Clean sentences changed:** "thirtieth" -> "thirtyeth" (a misspelling), "order the big bag of kibble please" ->
  "Please order...", "Eating takes me" -> "My eating takes", "add thickener" -> "add a thickener". big3s has 48
  casual-request examples ("please" left where it is); that wasn't enough to stop it moving "please".
- **Misheard words left as heard or replaced with a wrong word:** "curve side pickup", "pred knee zone", "thick
  inner", "sir trailing" -> "scheduling", "ought to pay" -> "my light". Most of these terms are ones big1 trained on
  and big3s didn't (the fair split leaves them out).
- **Won by big3s:** guest list, autopay, discharge plan, Medicare (twice; big1 wrote Medicaid), jury duty, crochet, and
  the clean "estimated taxes" (big1 added "the").

## What it means

- At about big1's size (3,153 different answers vs big1's 2,778), big3's mix at rank 32 doesn't beat big1's mix at
  rank 16. big2 showed the same. So rank 32, or something in the newer mix, costs a little, mostly on clean sentences
  and on spoken numbers. Unmeasured which one. A big3s run at rank 16 (about 3 h) would tell.
- big3 (24,000 examples, 5,752 different answers) has the same mix and recipe, and big3s is a random part of it. So
  big3 against big3s shows what three times the data does, with nothing else changed.
- The loss checks say the lever is what generalizes, not training longer: data with more different sentences, or
  something that slows memorizing (more dropout, a lower learning rate). Neither is tested yet.
