# big3: three times the data makes it worse

big3 is the overnight run of 2026-11-04/05: a LoRA adapter on Qwen3.5-4B, rank 32 / alpha 64, 2 epochs, the same
recipe as big3s. Data: `data/cleanup_train/big3_train.jsonl`, 24,000 examples with 5,752 different answers, 22,793 trained
and 1,207 held back as whole sentences. 5,698 steps, 427.8 min on the 5080 (peak 10.21 GB). big3s's 8,000 are a random
part of these 24,000, so **big3 against big3s is three times the data with nothing else changed.** Scoring as the app
runs it: llama.cpp b11960, Q8_0, greedy, prompt `cleanup_t1_short`, Khang's PC (RTX 5080). The test file is the
unchanged big1_test.csv (sha256 ec2b1b0aa648c0e3).

## The loss checks: it learns our generator, not the task

Loss with dropout off, every 500 steps (step 2,849 is the end of epoch 1):

| Step | 0 | 500 | 1,000 | 2,000 | 2,849 | 3,500 | 4,500 | 5,000 | 5,698 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Training examples | 0.0865 | 0.0161 | 0.0117 | 0.0131 | 0.0039 | 0.0021 | 0.0012 | 0.0009 | 0.0008 |
| Held-back sentences | 0.0878 | 0.0206 | 0.0150 | 0.0190 | 0.0156 | 0.0074 | 0.0108 | 0.0075 | 0.0067 |
| Test cases (400 of the 2,500) | 0.1062 | 0.0326 | 0.0347 | 0.0387 | 0.0339 | 0.0295 | 0.0337 | 0.0283 | 0.0279 |

- **Held-back sentences keep improving**, to 0.0067: far better than big3s's 0.0170 (a different held-back set of the
  same kind). On new sentences from **our own generator**, more data helps a lot.
- **The test cases stop improving at step 500.** From 0.0326 there, the loss wanders between 0.028 and 0.039 and
  ends at 0.0279: worse than big3s (0.0208) and big1 (0.0190). The test was written by big1's generator, with
  different terms. So everything after step 500 makes the model better at our data's habits, not at the job.
- A held-back sentence isn't really new: it comes from the same sentence banks and templates as the training data.
  The test-case loss is the honest live measure. Keep watching it in every run.

**big2 overfits in the textbook sense.** Its training loss falls tenfold after epoch 1 while its test-case loss
rises at every epoch. (Its automatic test score still rose, 2,362 to 2,411, so the loss and the pass rate don't always
agree. The loss also counts how sure the model is.) big1's test-case loss is the lowest, partly because big1 trained on
some of the test's terms.

## Why clean sentences get changed: the noise teaches "add the"

12 of big3's 15 changed clean sentences only add a small word: "for kids" -> "for the kids", "scraping on bumps" ->
"on the bumps", "pay for equipment" -> "for the equipment", "check your mic" -> "please check your mic". It's the
same on dev (C04 "pick up milk and bread" -> "pick up the milk and bread"). No earlier big run changed a dev clean
sentence (big1, big2, big3s: 0 of 10).

The cause is in `engine/voice/cleanup_train/noise.py`. A "broken" example (14% of every data set) is a clean sentence with
small words deleted (the, a, my, for, ...; each with chance 0.6, `drop_small_words`), and the answer puts them back. In
big3, 43% of the broken examples add a "the". The model can't tell "the was lost" from "the was never said". So it
learns to put "the" wherever one would fit, and "pay for equipment" looks like a sentence that lost one. The share is
the same in every data set (answers adding "the": big1 6.3%, big2 6.8%, big3s 6.9%, big3 6.8%). More data just
drives the same lesson in harder: clean changes go 7 (big3s) -> 15 (big3).

## What it means

1. **More examples from the same banks hurt.** Three times the data, with nothing else changed, lost on the test,
   the fair split, clean sentences and dev. Our "different sentences" are template fills, so they're new to the
   held-back loss but not new to the task. The way to 100,000 examples is new sentences from new sources, with no more
   noisy copies per sentence than now (2 to 3).
2. **Not underfitting, and capacity isn't the limit.** Every run learns its training data to about 0.001.
3. **Overfitting, to the generator.** Training past about 500 to 1,000 steps mostly memorizes our data's habits.
   big2's test-case loss rises with every epoch.
4. **Cheap next tests, at big3s's size (about 3 h each), judged by the test-case loss and the fair split:** clean
   examples where a "the" would fit but wasn't said (answer unchanged); a rarer drop of "the" and "a" in broken
   examples; rank 16; a lower learning rate or more dropout. Change one thing per run.
